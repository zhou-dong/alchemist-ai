# Facts — Domains of Life

*General facts about LUCA and the domains of life (Bacteria, Archaea, Eukaryotes), for any chapter to reference. Not chapter-specific — chapter factsheets should point here rather than duplicating.*

## LUCA

- LUCA (Last Universal Common Ancestor) is not itself a bacterium or an archaeon — it's the population that existed before Bacteria and Archaea diverged from each other. (established, by definition)
- LUCA is usually placed earlier than ~3.9 Gya, right around or before the Hadean/Archean boundary (~4.0 Gya). (plausible — inferred from comparative phylogenetics/molecular clock estimates, not direct fossil evidence; exact timing is debated)
- LUCA was very likely sessile (not motile) — flagella/archaella evolved independently after LUCA in each domain, per below. (plausible)

## Bacteria and Archaea

- Bacteria and Archaea are the two domains that diverged from LUCA. (established)
- Flagellar motility (bacterial flagella) and the archaeal equivalent (archaella) are not homologous — they evolved independently in the two domains, not from a single shared ancestral structure. (established)
- Flagella/archaella are generally treated as evolving after LUCA, not present in it — motility organelles are lineage-specific, later innovations. (plausible — inferred from comparative phylogenetics, not direct fossil evidence)
- Archaea still exist today as a living domain, not an extinct/ancestral-only group — found in extreme environments (hydrothermal vents, hot springs, salt lakes) and also common environments (soil, oceans, human gut microbiome). (established)

## Eukaryotes

- Eukaryotes did not branch off directly from LUCA the way Bacteria and Archaea did — they arose later, from a merger between the two existing domains. (established, per endosymbiotic theory)
- The best-supported model: an archaeon host took in (or fused with) a bacterium, which became the mitochondrion — the energy-producing structure still found in eukaryotic cells today. (established — endosymbiotic theory, strongly supported by mitochondrial DNA/genomic evidence)
- In plants and algae, a second, separate endosymbiotic event brought in a photosynthetic bacterium (cyanobacterium) that became the chloroplast. (established)
- The domain "tree" isn't a clean three-way split — Bacteria and Archaea diverge from LUCA, while Eukaryotes emerge later from a fusion between the two, not a third independent branch. (established)
