# Facts — Geologic Time Scale (Overview)

*General facts about how geologic time is divided, for any chapter to reference. Not chapter-specific.*

- The geologic time scale is a strict hierarchy of nested units, largest to smallest: **Eon → Era → Period → Epoch → Age.** (established — standard stratigraphic terminology, ICS/IUGS)
- There are four eons: **Hadean**, **Archean**, **Proterozoic**, and **Phanerozoic** (the current one). Eons are the largest division, spanning hundreds of millions to billions of years. (established)
- Eras only exist *within* the Phanerozoic eon (the last ~541 million years) — **Paleozoic**, **Mesozoic**, **Cenozoic** (current). The Hadean, Archean, and Proterozoic eons are not divided into eras in the standard timescale. (established)
- "Eon" and "era" are not interchangeable — an eon is the larger, top-level unit; an era is a subdivision that only applies to the Phanerozoic. For the Hadean and Archean specifically, "eon" is the only correct term. (established)
- The four eons in order (oldest to youngest): Hadean (~4.6–4.0 Gya) → Archean (~4.0–2.5 Gya) → Proterozoic (~2.5 Gya–541 Mya) → Phanerozoic (~541 Mya–present). (established)
