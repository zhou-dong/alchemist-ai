# Facts — Eukaryotes

*General facts about the origin of Eukaryotes, for any chapter to reference. Not chapter-specific.*

- Eukaryotes did not branch off directly from LUCA the way Bacteria and Archaea did — they arose later, from a merger between the two existing domains. (established, per endosymbiotic theory)
- The best-supported model: an archaeon host took in (or fused with) a bacterium, which became the mitochondrion — the energy-producing structure still found in eukaryotic cells today. (established — endosymbiotic theory, strongly supported by mitochondrial DNA/genomic evidence)
- In plants and algae, a second, separate endosymbiotic event brought in a photosynthetic bacterium (cyanobacterium) that became the chloroplast. (established)
