# Facts — The First Thing Alive

*See `shared.md` for facts this chapter depends on but doesn't own (motility timing, chemotaxis signaling conservation, early metabolism, vent setting). This file holds only facts specific to this chapter.*

## Setting

- This chapter is set earlier than Chapter 0 — before any free-swimming, chemotaxis-driven organism, at a deep-sea hydrothermal vent rather than a shallow sunlit ocean. (plausible, per `shared.md` § Setting)
- No sunlight reaches this environment; it has never depended on solar energy. (established — physical fact of ocean depth and light attenuation)

## Organism

- The organism is sessile — fixed/attached to a rock surface at the vent mouth, not motile. (plausible, per `shared.md` § Motility)
- It has no flagella, no motor, no propulsion structure of any kind. (plausible — consistent with flagella being a post-LUCA, lineage-specific innovation per `shared.md` § Motility)
- It reproduces by division (splitting into two), the general mechanism by which single-celled life multiplies. (established as a general fact of cellular life; not vent-specific)

## Mechanism

- Energy source: hydrogen (H₂) and hydrogen sulfide (H₂S) dissolved in vent fluid, used as electron donors in redox chemistry. (plausible, per `shared.md` § Early metabolism)
- Carbon source: CO₂ fixed from the surrounding water, not organic molecules. (plausible, per `shared.md` § Early metabolism)
- The reaction is local and surface-based — chemistry contacting the organism's surface directly, with no evidence for or need of any internal signal propagation across the organism at this stage. (speculative — this is a narrative simplification for pedagogical purposes, not a specific claim about a documented biochemical pathway)
